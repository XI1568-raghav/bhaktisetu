# Kashi Vishwanath Live Darshan: How to Take Darshan of the Jyotirlinga from Anywhere

For anyone who has stood barefoot in the gullies of *Banaras* before sunrise, the question is not whether *Kashi Vishwanath live darshan* online can match that morning. It cannot. The honest question: when you cannot be there, can a screen still carry a real *darshan* of *Mahadev*? Yes — if three things are true. The stream is live, not a recording. The temple area and timings are verified. And if a priest is performing a *puja* in your name, you receive video proof it happened. This guide explains how that works for Kashi, with [Darshya](/about) as one option among several.

## What makes Kashi Vishwanath unique

Kashi Vishwanath is one of the twelve *Jyotirlingas* — the self-manifested *lingams* of Shiva named in the *Shiva Purana*. The temple stands on the western bank of the Ganga in *Varanasi*, a few minutes' walk from Manikarnika ghat, the cremation ghat where Hindus have brought their dead for at least two and a half millennia. That proximity is not incidental. The local theology holds that Shiva himself whispers the *Taraka mantra* into the ear of those who die at Kashi, granting *moksha*. The temple, the ghat, and the city are read as one continuous sacred geography.

The current temple, with its gold-plated *shikhara*, was rebuilt by Maharani Ahilyabai Holkar of Indore in 1780. The earlier structure had been demolished in 1669 under Aurangzeb; the Gyanvapi Mosque, built immediately adjacent on part of the original footprint, remains visible to anyone who walks the corridor today.

*Adi Shankaracharya* visited Kashi in the 8th century and is traditionally credited with composing the *Kashi Panchakam* and several Shiva stotras here, including verses still chanted in the daily *aarti*. The Kashi Vishwanath Corridor, opened in December 2021, widened access between the temple and the Ganga ghats. For a devotee watching from Houston or Hyderabad, this matters because authentic live streams originate from the corridor and approach areas — not from inside the sanctum, where cameras are not permitted.

## The ritual: what happens during a Kashi darshan

Kashi runs on a fixed daily clock. Times are IST and shift slightly on festivals; treat the table below as the standard non-festival schedule.

| Ritual | Time (IST) | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| *Mangala aarti* | 03:00 – 04:00 | The *lingam* is woken with conch, bell, and lamp; ticketed entry only |
| *Bhog aarti* | 11:15 – 12:20 | Mid-morning food offering |
| *Sapta Rishi aarti* | 19:00 – 20:15 | Evening aarti named for the seven sages |
| *Shringar aarti* | 21:00 – 22:15 | The *lingam* is dressed and adorned |
| *Shayan aarti* | 22:30 – 23:00 (approx.) | Closing aarti before the doors shut |

Between aartis, the temple is open for general *darshan* and for *abhishek* — the ritual bathing of the *lingam* with water from the Ganga, milk, curd, ghee, honey, and sugar (the *panchamrit*). A devotee booking an *abhishek* for themselves or for parents typically chooses one of these slots, states a *sankalp* (formal intention naming the devotee, their *gotra*, and the purpose of the prayer), and the priest performs the rite.

The *sandhya aarti* at dusk, when the lamps come up along the riverfront and the conches sound across the corridor, is what most NRI devotees say they want to see. It is also, in practice, the slot that streams most reliably — the light is good, the camera angles are stable, and the audio carries.

## How to attend live darshan online

There is no single "official" Kashi Vishwanath live stream URL that the Shri Kashi Vishwanath Temple Trust publishes for global use; cameras are restricted inside the *garbha griha* itself, and what most platforms broadcast is from the temple corridor, the entrance, and the Manikarnika ghat side. Be skeptical of any service that promises a live feed of the *lingam* itself.

What you can reliably watch online:

- The Kashi Vishwanath Corridor and approach to the temple
- Ganga aarti at Dashashwamedh ghat (about 800 m away) — streamed widely
- Manikarnika ghat ambient feeds
- Temple priests performing *abhishek* on behalf of a specific devotee, with that priest on camera
