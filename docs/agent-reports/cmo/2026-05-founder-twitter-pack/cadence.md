# Posting Cadence — Month 5 (4 weeks × 3 posts/week)

## Slot schedule

| Day | Time IST | Time PT (day before) | Audience reached |
|-----|----------|----------------------|------------------|
| Mon | 09:00    | Sun 20:30 (prev day) | India morning commute + NRI Sunday evening US-West |
| Wed | 09:00    | Tue 20:30            | India midweek + NRI Tuesday evening |
| Fri | 09:00    | Thu 20:30            | India weekend prep + NRI Thursday evening |

> 9:00 IST = the sweet spot that catches India morning scroll and the US-West evening window the night before. Same time slot all 4 weeks — predictability beats optimization at this scale.

- **Week 1 (foundation):** W1-1 origin (Mon) → W1-2 stack (Wed) → W1-3 first 10 customers (Fri)
- **Week 2 (product depth):** W2-1 temple bookings (Mon) → W2-2 darshan latency (Wed) → W2-3 pricing (Fri)
- **Week 3 (numbers + transparency):** W3-1 MRR (Mon) → W3-2 funnel (Wed) → W3-3 NRI gift puja (Fri)

## Queue prep workflow (Sunday night)

1. Sunday 21:00 IST: read this week's 3 threads from the pack.
2. Rewrite each tweet line in your own voice (the agent draft is a scaffold).
3. Verify every tweet is ≤280 chars (paste into Typefully or X composer — both warn).
4. Schedule all 3 for the week in Typefully / Buffer / native X scheduler.
5. Drop a calendar reminder for Mon/Wed/Fri at 09:30 IST — reply window opens (see `engagement-playbook.md`).

## Holiday + festival flex

- If a thread date falls on a major Hindu festival (Diwali, Holi, Janmashtami, etc.), shift to the next non-festival weekday and post a one-tweet festival greeting in the original slot instead.
- If the founder is travelling and can't honor the <2h reply SLA, delay the thread by one slot — silent threads underperform versus engaged ones.

## What NOT to do

- Don't post more than one thread per day — saturates your follower feed.
- Don't post after 21:00 IST — India audience has scrolled past, NRI not yet awake.
- Don't auto-DM new followers — kills credibility (see playbook).
- Don't engage in tribal arguments (politics, religion-vs-religion debates) — even if the bait is irresistible. Bhaktisetu is devotional infrastructure, not a sect.
