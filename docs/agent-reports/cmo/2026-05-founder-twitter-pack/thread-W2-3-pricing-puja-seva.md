1/9 Pricing puja seva — what is fair? A thread on how Bhaktisetu sets prices, why we refuse some bookings, and the one number we will never lie about. <!-- 152/280 -->

2/9 The honest decomposition of a ₹2100 puja booking: pandit dakshina ₹1200, samagri ₹400, temple commission ₹250, platform commission ₹250. We publish this on the booking page. Devotees deserve the breakdown. <!-- 235/280 -->

3/9 We will never charge more for an "auspicious time" than what the temple authority sets. Some aggregators 3x the price on Mahashivratri. We don't. Same Rudrabhishek on a normal Monday vs Mahashivratri: same base price. <!-- 244/280 -->

4/9 What we do charge extra for: NRI bookings in USD. Reason: forex, payment processor fees, and the genuine ops cost of WhatsApp + video proof delivery. NRI pays roughly 9-10x domestic. We disclose this on the gift puja page. <!-- 252/280 -->

5/9 The "fair" question gets harder for famous temples. A Tirupati seva via TTD is ₹500. The same seva via a Vrindavan-trained pandit is ₹3500. Both are valid. We let the devotee choose, with clear pandit-credential cards. <!-- 256/280 -->

6/9 Bookings we refuse: anything where the devotee asks for a "guaranteed wish-fulfilment". We don't sell outcomes. We sell the ritual. The phala (fruit) of any puja is between the devotee and the deity — not us, not the pandit. <!-- 251/280 -->

7/9 The one number we will never lie about: did the puja happen, with the right gotra, at the right muhurat, by the named pandit. Video proof is the contract. If any of those four are wrong, we refund without asking. <!-- 234/280 -->

8/9 Pricing controversy from last month: a customer asked why our Lakshmi puja was ₹1100 when a local temple does it for ₹251. Answer: we wrap pandit + samagri + booking + video. The local temple charges only dakshina. Both are honest. <!-- 257/280 -->

9/9 If you've ever wondered whether an online puja price is fair — read the breakdown on the booking page. We show every rupee. bhaktisetu.com/pujas <!-- 158/280 -->
