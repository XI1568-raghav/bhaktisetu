1/9 How temple bookings actually work in India — a thread for anyone who thinks "temple seva online" is a solved problem. It is very much not. 1/9 <!-- 152/280 -->

2/9 Most temples in India do not have a booking system. They have a counter. The counter has a register. The register has a phone number. That is the booking system. <!-- 167/280 -->

3/9 The "online" alternatives split into 3 buckets: (a) state-run portals (TTD, Shirdi, Vaishno Devi) — these mostly work, (b) aggregator apps that scrape phone numbers — these mostly do not, (c) WhatsApp groups run by individual pandits. <!-- 248/280 -->

4/9 The aggregator failure mode: you pay online. The aggregator calls the temple. The temple has no slot. The aggregator either delays you, swaps the temple silently, or refunds 7 days later. The pandit gets ₹0. <!-- 220/280 -->

5/9 Our approach: we only onboard a temple after a video call with the temple authority + a written commission agreement (20-40% to platform, rest direct to pandit). If they don't agree to video proof, we don't onboard them. <!-- 250/280 -->

6/9 Why video proof matters more in India than online media admits: middle-class Indian families are skeptical of any online religious service after the Patanjali / fake-pandit scandals of 2018-22. Trust must be re-earned, not assumed. <!-- 251/280 -->

7/9 What we got wrong in year 1: we tried to onboard 50 temples in 90 days. Result: 6 honored bookings, 3 ghosted, 41 sat idle. In year 2 we onboarded 18 temples carefully — all 18 are still active. Depth beats breadth. <!-- 245/280 -->

8/9 Pricing varies wildly. Rudrabhishek at a small Pune temple: ₹751. Same puja at a Varanasi famous-name temple: ₹5100. We show both, label them honestly, let the devotee choose. We don't pretend they're the same product. <!-- 257/280 -->

9/9 The unsexy truth: temple-tech is mostly trust-tech and ops-tech. Booking flows are 10% of the problem. Pandit relationships are 90%. Try a verified seva → bhaktisetu.com/pujas <!-- 197/280 -->
