2/9 Disclaimer first: I am not a CPA. This is a founder explaining what we ship + what users ask. Confirm your specifics with a US tax pro. The structural facts below are public knowledge. <!-- 207/280 -->

3/9 Section 80G is an Indian tax deduction. It applies to Indian income tax filers, not US taxpayers. A US-only filer sending USD to an Indian temple does NOT get a US deduction just because the Indian charity has 80G registration. <!-- 257/280 -->

4/9 Two valid US tax scenarios: (a) you file in both US + India (rare), (b) the receiving Indian organization has US 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsorship via a Friends-of-X structure. Most temples do not have this. We disclose which do. <!-- 271/280 -->

5/9 What 80G receipts ARE good for: (a) Indian-tax-filing family members who can use the deduction (you donate, parent claims), (b) record-keeping for personal tracking, (c) proving the donation reached the registered donee + PAN. <!-- 263/280 -->

6/9 What our 80G receipt includes: donee 80G registration number, donee PAN, donor name + PAN (you provide), amount in INR, date, and a unique receipt number DR-YYYYMMDD-XXXX. Honest paper trail. <!-- 211/280 -->

7/9 The most common NRI misconception: "if it has 80G, I get US deduction". No. The temple needs US 501(c)(3) status (separately) for that. The 80G stamp is for Indian filers. We say so on every donation page. <!-- 248/280 -->

8/9 The valid use case we see most: NRI sons + daughters donating to ancestral temples in their parents' name. Parent claims the 80G in India. NRI gets the karma + a beautiful receipt. Everyone honest. <!-- 220/280 -->
