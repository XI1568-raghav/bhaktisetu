1/8 The NRI gift puja was a side feature. It became our #1 SKU by revenue. Here's what we learned about how diaspora devotees actually buy. <!-- 144/280 -->

2/8 Original hypothesis: NRIs would book pujas for themselves in their cities of origin. Reality: 76% of NRI bookings are gift pujas — daughter in Toronto buying for mother in Bhopal, son in Sydney for father in Chennai. <!-- 247/280 -->

3/8 The occasion mix is sharper than I expected. Birthdays of parents: 34%. Anniversaries: 21%. Shradh (death anniversary): 18%. Festival-specific gift seva: 14%. "Just because" donations: 13%. <!-- 215/280 -->

4/8 The unlock was WhatsApp delivery to two recipients: the giver gets a confirmation in USD with the booking number, the recipient parent gets a Hindi/English message with the puja video. Same flow, two audiences. <!-- 245/280 -->

5/8 Pricing: NRI gift puja is 9-10x domestic. NRIs do not flinch at $50-90 for a ritual that costs ₹500-900 locally. They are paying for trust + delivery + the relief of not having to coordinate from 12 time zones away. <!-- 268/280 -->

6/8 The hardest part: the recipient parent often doesn't have WhatsApp set up, or uses a feature phone. We added an SMS fallback with a hosted-video link that works on any phone. ~12% of recipients use the SMS link. <!-- 238/280 -->

7/8 The unexpected channel: family WhatsApp groups. ~22% of new NRI users say "my cousin sent me the video, I asked where it was from". Devotional commerce spreads through families, not feeds. <!-- 215/280 -->
