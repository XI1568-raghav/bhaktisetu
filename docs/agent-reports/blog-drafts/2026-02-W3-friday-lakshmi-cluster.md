## Why Lakshmi on Friday

In the Sanatana tradition, each day of the week is dedicated to a specific devata — a divine archetype whose energy is said to be most accessible on that vaar. Friday belongs to Lakshmi, the embodiment of Vedic hymn. The Skanda Purana and the Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra both reference the vaar devata system, codifying which planetary lord governs each weekday and which deity's puja yields the most fruit. For Lakshmi specifically, the Friday association traces back to scripture: the Shiva Purana for Monday Shiva worship, the Hanuman Chalisa tradition for Tuesday and Saturday Hanuman, the Ganesh Atharvashirsha for Wednesday Ganesha, the Vishnu Purana and Bhagavad Gita for Thursday Vishnu, the Sri Suktam for Friday Lakshmi, and the Aditya Hridayam from the Ramayana for Sunday Surya.

This week — Week 3 of our Daily Devata cluster — we focus on **Sri Suktam**. Whether you are a daily sadhak or a once-a-week devotee, performing Lakshmi puja on Friday carries amplified merit (punya). The vibrational alignment between the planetary ruler, the deity, and the devotee's intention is said to compound on this day.

## Vidhi: Step-by-Step Lakshmi Puja for Friday

A typical household Lakshmi puja on Friday follows this sequence:

1. **Snan and Achaman** — Bathe before sunrise. Wear clean, preferably white or saffron clothes.
2. **Sankalpa** — State your intention out loud. Include your name, gotra, location, and the purpose of the puja.
3. **Asana shuddhi** — Sit on a wool or kusha grass mat facing east or north.
4. **Kalash sthapana** — Place a brass or copper kalash filled with water, mango leaves, and a coconut on a small platform.
5. **Avahana** — Invite Lakshmi into the murti or yantra using the appropriate avahana mantra.
6. **Panchopachara** — Offer the five basic upacharas: gandha (sandalwood paste), pushpa (flowers), dhupa (incense), deepa (lamp), naivedya (food offering).
7. **Mantra japa** — Chant **Om Shreem Mahalakshmiyei Namah** 108 times using a tulsi or rudraksha mala.
8. **Aarti** — Conclude with the deity's aarti, circling the lamp clockwise.
9. **Visarjan** — Respectfully request the deity to return, and distribute prasad.

## Mantras and Aarti

The core mantra for Friday's Lakshmi worship is:

**Om Shreem Mahalakshmiyei Namah**

Recite this mantra 108 times. If you prefer a shorter practice, even 11 or 21 repetitions with full attention (chitta-ekagra) is considered more potent than mechanical mass repetition.

The aarti is the emotional crescendo of the puja. Sing the [Lakshmi aarti](/aarti/lakshmi-aarti) with bhava — devotional feeling matters more than perfect pronunciation. Many devotees keep a printed aarti book or use a devotional app to follow along.

## Stories and Leelas

The scriptures preserve countless leelas (divine plays) of Lakshmi. One frequently retold story explains why Friday became sacred to this devata. In the Puranic narrative, Lakshmi's grace was first sought on a Friday by a great rishi or king who attained moksha, prosperity, or freedom from suffering as a result. The pattern repeats across regional traditions — Tamil, Bengali, Marathi, and North Indian retellings each emphasize different aspects of the same core teaching.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I perform Lakshmi puja on Friday without a pandit?**
Yes. A simple household puja with sankalpa, panchopachara, mantra japa, and aarti is valid and meritorious. Pandit-led pujas are recommended for life-event rituals, vrats, or when you want the full Vedic vidhi performed with precision and video proof.

**Q: What if I miss the morning muhurta?**
Lakshmi puja can be performed at any time on Friday. The morning Brahma muhurta is most auspicious, but a sincere evening puja with full bhava is far better than a rushed dawn ritual. Pradosh time (twilight) is also considered favorable for most devatas.

**Q: Is fasting mandatory for Friday Lakshmi worship?**
Fasting is recommended for serious sadhana but not mandatory. A satvik diet, abstaining from onion and garlic and non-vegetarian food on Friday, is the minimum discipline most householders follow. Phalahar (fruits and milk) fasts are common for those observing the full vrat.
