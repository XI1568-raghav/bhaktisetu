The **Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad** (महानारायणोपनिषद्) is a long, composite text appearing as the tenth and final *prapāṭhaka* of the **Taittirīya Āraṇyaka** of the Krishna Yajurveda. Sometimes counted among the Vaishnava Upanishads, it is in fact a comprehensive ritual-philosophical anthology — a late Vedic compilation that gathers in one place many of the mantras most widely used in daily Smarta liturgy.

## Two Major Recensions

The Upanishad exists in two principal recensions of substantially different length. The **Āndhra** recension, predominant in south India, has 64 *anuvākas* in some manuscripts and is the basis of most printed editions. The **Drāviḍa** recension, used by Tamil-tradition reciters, contains roughly 80 anuvākas. The variation is largely a question of which auxiliary mantra sequences are included and in what order; the philosophical core is shared.

## A Liturgical Anthology

What makes the Mahanarayana distinctive is that it functions as a working liturgical handbook. Almost every important Vedic prayer used in the daily *sandhyāvandanam*, the *agnihotra*, the *brahmayajña*, and the rites of *prāyaścitta* (atonement) is drawn from this Upanishad. Among the texts it preserves:

- The **Nārāyaṇa Anuvāka** — the source of the famous formulae *oṃ namo nārāyaṇāya* and *yo vai vedādau svaraḥ prokto*.
- The **Sandhyā mantras** — the formal invocations of dawn and dusk worship.
- The **Aghamarṣaṇa Sūkta** — for the dispelling of sin.
- The **Medhāsūkta** — for sharpness of intellect, recited by students.
- The **Pavamāna mantras** — for purification, including *asato mā sadgamaya*.
- The **Durga Sūkta**, **Śrī Sūkta** (in some recensions), and **Bhū Sūkta**.
- The full **Pañcāyatana** invocation of the five deities — Shiva, Vishnu, Surya, Devi, Ganesha — central to Smarta worship.

## The Narayana Center

The theological center of the Upanishad is the identification of the supreme reality as **Nārāyaṇa** (नारायण). One of the most-recited passages declares:

नारायणं महाज्ञेयं विश्वात्मानं परायणम् ।
*nārāyaṇaṃ mahājñeyaṃ viśvātmānaṃ parāyaṇam*
"Narayana is the great Knowable, the Self of the universe, the supreme refuge."

This Narayana is at the same time conceived as the *Puruṣa* of the Purusha Sukta, identified with **brahman**, and invoked under many names. The Upanishad is a major textual support for the Vaishnava theology of Narayana as the supreme reality, but its content is broad enough that Shaiva and Shakta worshippers also draw from it.

## The Yajnopavita and Bhasma Mantras

For Smarta Brahmins the Mahanarayana is the source of the mantras spoken at putting on the **yajñopavīta** (sacred thread) and at applying **bhasma** (sacred ash) or **tilak**. Every initiated Brahmin therefore recites portions of this Upanishad daily — often without realizing that they come from a single ancient text.

## A Famous Pavamana Mantra

Among the most beloved verses preserved here:

ॐ असतो मा सद्गमय । तमसो मा ज्योतिर्गमय । मृत्योर्मा अमृतं गमय ।
*oṃ asato mā sadgamaya, tamaso mā jyotirgamaya, mṛtyor mā amṛtaṃ gamaya*
"From the unreal lead me to the real; from darkness lead me to light; from death lead me to immortality."

Although usually quoted from the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.3.28, the prayer recurs in the Mahanarayana and is its most widely known fragment in the contemporary world.

## Place in the Tradition

Modern scholars (Olivelle, Varenne, Witzel) date the Mahanarayana relatively late among the Mukhya Upanishads — likely composed during the last centuries BCE — and view it as a deliberately encyclopedic compilation. The medieval Vedantic commentators paid it less attention than the older Upanishads, but its liturgical influence has been incalculable. To recite the *sandhyāvandanam* in any orthodox South Indian tradition is, line by line, to recite the Mahanarayana — the Upanishad that quietly underwrites the daily devotional life of millions.
